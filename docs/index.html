The central insight

Aggregate reward guidance
before updating the model.

Reward-guided diffusion training needs more than a strong reward signal. It also needs a useful update direction, and a mechanism that keeps that update stable.

What makes NFT unstable?

Our analysis identifies a hidden forward–reverse consistency force in practical NFT-style training, in addition to the intended reward guidance. Positive and negative branches can cancel this force, but that cancellation is not controlled at each state.

Why does off-policy training help?

A lagged reference preserves a restoration gradient toward the previous policy. The stabilizing effect is not just about which samples are generated: the old-policy anchor matters.

A different order of operations

DiffusionART first aggregates endpoint rewards into one direction per prompt, then applies that direction to rollout states. Frozen-target replay supplies adaptive restoration through a second MSE update.

Aggregation-first reward tilting

From a group of samples to a shared update direction.

  1. 1

    Sample & score

    Generate K endpoints for a prompt using the current policy. Score them with scalar or pairwise feedback.

  2. 2

    Aggregate first

    Compute reward–endpoint covariance, then normalize the aggregated direction.

    G(c) = normalize(Cov(R, x0 | c))

  3. 3

    Guide & restore

    Freeze the target and fit it on two disjoint minibatches. The second update adds restoration as the model moves.

    vtarget = vold + G(c)

One pair can be enough.

Pairwise DiffusionART learns from binary preferences, without using reward magnitudes as guidance. With two videos per prompt, it reaches VQ 12.5 in 2.8× fewer GPU hours than K = 8 in the reported T2V setting.

Beyond text-to-video.

Experiments also cover text-to-image and image-to-video generation. On distilled Wan2.1-I2V-14B, DiffusionART reaches MQ 6 with 5.6× fewer GPU hours than Flow-GRPO-Fast.
